## Proposed structure
Three linked scales: each chapter resolves an incident; recurring crew relationships continue across chapters; the protagonist's responsibilities grow over a multi-year career. Repeat discovery -> provisional understanding -> meaningful investigation -> evidence that changes the interpretation -> action -> aftermath. Not every chapter needs a twist, danger escalation or a morality exam. Calm, amusing, exploratory and serious chapters alternate.
A chapter is designed from: actual world situation, opening question, player's legitimate role, available evidence, consequences of actions, satisfying partial/complete resolution, and one durable relationship or responsibility change. Choose devices only after these.
Growth is widening responsibility, not clicks or rank XP. Cadet protagonist is not a uniquely gifted savior; specialists retain competence, perform other work, disagree reasonably, and integrate the player's assigned contribution. Player personality is not pre-fixed as arrogant or approval-seeking.
Begin on Enterprise-D. Later commissions, assignments and time skips establish the career; eventual captaincy is of the player's own ship, not automatically replacing Picard. Original fan scenario, not a canon Academy curriculum or canonical promotion schedule.

## Provisional chapter map
1. 返事をする海 — cadet science rotation; investigate an apparent response from an unknown ocean. Observation versus interpretation, comparisons, reporting limits. Story payoff: an apparently addressed signal becomes the activity of an ecosystem.
2. 地図にない帰り道 — supervised navigation assignment; two ships' position reports disagree and a rendezvous must be established. Reference frames, motion, measurement uncertainty, explaining one's location.
3. 船内の小さな異邦人 — engineering/medical/science cooperation; an unfamiliar passenger's environmental needs conflict with the crew's assumptions. Life support, environment, respecting individual needs, asking experts. Avoid making a person simply a resource-management obstacle.
4. 同じ記録、違う証言 — junior officer; contradictory incident reports reflect different viewpoints and timings. Data provenance, verification, honest reporting, colleague trust.
5. はじめて率いる班 — small survey team; divided observations and weather/terrain conditions require a shared plan. Delegation, communications, contingency planning and bringing everyone back.
6. 助けることの条件 — experienced officer; a settlement requests help but parties disagree about what help means. Consent, culture, diplomacy, technical options and consequences.
7. 艦橋を預かる夜 — supervised command experience; overlapping obligations and imperfect reports require prioritization. Integrating specialist advice, explaining orders, responsibility. Serious stakes only after relevant foundations.
8. あなたの航海 — later captain appointment and first mission on own ship. An unfamiliar situation evokes but does not repeat early lessons; commander must enable others to investigate and take responsibility for the chosen course.
These are chapter functions, not eight completed scenarios or eight lessons guaranteeing qualification. Multiple stories and years may occur within stages. Chapters 2-4 can be reordered; team command precedes ship command. Gradually reuse concepts in different circumstances, rather than one topic once.

## Recommended first chapter: 返事をする海 (author-facing outline)
Opening: during an ordinary survey, Enterprise's probe transmits a routine active survey pulse; rings of light appear on the ocean below. A junior crewmate wonders whether it is a reply. Senior science staff keep several hypotheses open: instrument effect, nonliving response, living response, intentional communication. The task is to establish what occurred before recommending a next contact action.
Legitimate player role: pair a short local sequence of images with the probe's transmission log under a mentor. Other personnel examine spectra, environment and communication patterns. The same instruments work for everyone. New knowledge comes from comparing conditions and pooling observations, not the player's magical scan or incompetent senior officers.
World truth fixed for the proposed fictional scenario: ocean organisms can relay luminescence after a stimulus; active survey emissions can initiate a local cascade, while natural stimuli elsewhere also initiate cascades. This is invented alien biology, not a known real mechanism. Exact sensing/interaction physics requires consistency review before implementation.
Investigation: a passive view avoids adding a new stimulus and can reveal independent waves elsewhere; time-aligned records distinguish the probe pulse from later propagation; wider views show propagation through patches rather than a planet-wide instant reply. Each action has an understandable information purpose; never just press scan until a counter completes. A second active stimulus, if offered, needs scientific justification and senior authorization; do not require risky repeated probing to solve the story.
Evidence discipline: natural waves do not logically disprove intelligence, and stimulus response alone proves neither life nor intention. The science team may support a biological explanation from independent observations; the report must distinguish actual response, biological hypothesis/evidence, and unestablished intentional communication. Do not overclaim discovery solely from pattern.
Resolution: the crew understands enough to switch to passive survey and change the survey record, while questions about the organisms remain. A wide view reveals luminous wavefronts across the ocean even after our transmissions cease: the planet has its own ongoing activity, not merely a reaction to the hero.
Character beat: the excited colleague's first interpretation is refined without humiliation; the player's comparison is acknowledged as one contribution. A later invitation to join a survey briefing is a credible increase in participation, not instant promotion.
Interactive consequence: the sequence of comparisons changes which claims can be supported, crew dialogue and report certainty, not whether a prewritten narrator declares success. Essential evidence must remain discoverable; mentor assistance avoids a mandatory quiz. A lower-evidence report can honestly recommend further observation.
Theme: encounter unknown worlds before categorizing them as messages meant for us. Start with wonder and belonging. No heat/battery mini-game in this first chapter.
This is the selected development proposal, not a user-approved final script. Next permitted step is script/evidence consistency refinement, not code or site deployment.

## Beginning and ending strategy
Recommend start with the first discovery, not Borg disaster or forced trauma. Familiar crew and a valued ship must exist before their loss carries weight. If a future-captain prologue is wanted later, use a short restrained bridge moment then return to cadet days; do not claim severe trauma causes instant mastery.
Build each chapter's truth/evidence/outcome before UI. Keep maps and visuals small. Preserve resource-conscious bounded work; no broad parallel agent tasks.
